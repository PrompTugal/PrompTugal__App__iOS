How it was made

Seven sessions and a conductor

Each session owned a part of the app: platform and CI, the vault, team features, design and native screens, collaboration and AI, release, and this site. They worked on their own branches against a shared set of contracts, and a conductor merged their work into dev about every 30 minutes once CI was green. The night is written up checkpoint by checkpoint in the build log, and each session’s own account is in the overnight brief pack.

The app, like the PrompTugal sites, is powered by RiskMandate, the insurability layer for agentic AI. Its team data, keys and message lanes live in encrypted vaults made with sgit, which the app now reads and writes natively. What makes a team of more than 100 agents workable is RiskMandate’s Agent Behaviour Policies: every agent has a published policy saying what it can reach, what it was asked to do, and the gap between the two. The seven sessions that built the app worked the same way, each inside a written contract of what it owned.

The night cost about $332 in model usage across the eight sessions, plus macOS CI minutes. Runner queues of 20 to 60 minutes were the main constraint.
