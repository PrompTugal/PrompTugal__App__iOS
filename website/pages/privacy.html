Privacy · PrompTugal app

What the app keeps, what it sends, and to whom

PrompTugal {{version}} has no account system, no analytics, no advertising and no tracking. It has no server of its own that collects data about you. The public app holds no team data and no messages between people. Some features send what you write to other services, and only when you use them. This page lists every one.

What stays on your device

  • Your schedule, saved sessions and cached feeds, in the app’s private storage, so the app works offline.
  • Your settings and your reminder times.
  • Reminders. They are local notifications, scheduled on the device. Nothing is sent to a push server. The app asks for notification permission only after you tap to turn reminders on.
  • Website data. Cookies and storage of the websites you open in the app, one separate store per website, kept apart from Safari.
  • The Home Screen widget and the Siri and Shortcuts answers. The app keeps a small copy of what is on now and next (session titles, rooms, times, and whether you picked the session) in storage shared only with its own widget and Siri extension on this device. They read that copy. They do not use the network and they cannot open your vault.
  • The camera, only when you tap Scan to read the QR code of a vault key. The code is read on the device, and the camera picture is not saved or sent.
  • Your Ask conversation, only if you turn on “Keep the conversation on this device”. It is off by default.

Deleting the app removes all of it from the device.

What the app sends, and where

Public feeds
The agenda, the news and the graph come from promptugal.news. These are reads of public files. The app sends nothing about you with them.
Ask (AI chat)
When you use Ask, your question, the conversation so far and the kinds of context you switch on are sent with your own key to openrouter.ai, which passes them to the maker of the AI model you chose. Before the first message, Ask shows what it sends and to whom, and sends nothing until you tap Allow. Each kind of context has its own switch; only the public agenda is on by default. You can withdraw permission in Ask → Key and settings.
Your vault
If you set up a personal vault, its reads and writes go to the vault server, dev.send.sgraph.ai. Everything is encrypted on the device before it leaves; the server stores ciphertext and opaque file names. What the app may write to your vault: your picks, notes, and your OpenRouter key if you choose to save it there. You do not need a vault to use the app.
Calendar
“Add to Calendar” writes a session to your calendar, only when you ask, with write-only access. The app cannot read your calendar.
The websites you open
Pages load from their own sites, as in a browser. Each site’s privacy policy covers what it does. Links that leave a site open in Safari.

Where the keys are kept

  • Vault keys are in the Keychain on this device only, behind Face ID (or your device passcode where Face ID is not set up), and wrapped by a Secure Enclave key where the device has one. They are never synced to iCloud.
  • Your OpenRouter key is in the Keychain on this device only, readable after the first unlock. It is not behind Face ID, so Ask can work without a prompt, and it is never synced to iCloud.
  • No key appears in logs or crash reports.

What the app never does

  • No tracking, no analytics, no advertising, and no third-party SDKs: the app uses Apple’s frameworks only.
  • It does not sell or share data with data brokers, and it does not use your data to follow you across other apps or websites.
  • It does not read your contacts, photos, location or calendar.

The team’s own app

The PrompTugal team also has an app of its own, for its people, its agents and its messages. It is a different app with a different bundle identifier, and it is not in the App Store. Its privacy page is in the developer area. Nothing from it is in this app.

The beta

The beta is installed from Xcode or through TestFlight. If you join through TestFlight, Apple shares your email address, device model, OS version and any feedback or crash reports you send with the team. Apple’s TestFlight privacy notice has the details.

Checking this, and questions
