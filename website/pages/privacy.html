Privacy · PrompTugal.news app

The app collects no data

PrompTugal.news {{version}} has no account, no analytics, no advertising, no tracking and no third-party SDKs. Nothing you do in the app is sent to us or to anyone else. On the App Store its privacy label reads Data Not Collected.

What stays on your device

  • Your towns and settings, in the app's private storage.
  • A saved copy of the news and place data, so the app works with no signal. A copy is also built into the app for the first launch, and every screen says which copy it shows.
  • Your location, only if you allow it. "Near me" asks for location while the app is in use and works out the nearest town on the phone. The location is never sent anywhere and never stored.
  • Apple Intelligence summaries. On devices that have it, summaries are made on the device with Apple's on-device model. Nothing is sent to a server for them.
  • The reading meter, if shown, is a count kept on the device. Nothing is charged in this version.

Deleting the app removes all of it from the device.

What the app does on the network

Public news and place data
The app downloads public files from promptugal.news and its data sites (such as data.promptugal.news), from ios.app.promptugal.news, and public weather data from IPMA (api.ipma.pt). These are plain reads of public files, without cookies and without any identifier: nothing about you is sent.
Links
Every story links to its source. Sources and other websites open in Safari, under that site's own privacy policy.

What the app never does

  • No account, no tracking, no analytics, no advertising, and no third-party SDKs: the app uses Apple's frameworks only.
  • It does not send your location, your towns or your settings anywhere, and it has no server of its own that collects data about you.
  • It does not use the camera, microphone, contacts or photos, and it does not read your calendar.
  • It does not sell or share data, and it does not follow you across other apps or websites.

The beta

Test builds are installed through TestFlight. Apple then shares your email address, device model, OS version and any feedback or crash reports you choose to send with the team. Apple's TestFlight privacy notice.
