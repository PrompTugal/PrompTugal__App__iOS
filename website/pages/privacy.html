Privacy

What the app keeps, and where

PrompTugal {{version}} has no account, no analytics, no advertising and no server of its own that holds your data. It collects nothing about you.

What the app requests

  • Public PrompTugal.news feeds. The offline reader downloads the live agenda, event records and the news feed from promptugal.news. They are the same files anyone can open in a browser.
  • The websites you open. The website tabs load the sites in your sidebar, like a browser would. Each site’s own privacy policy covers what it does. Links that leave a site open in Safari.
  • Team data ({{status_team_dashboards}}). Team dashboards read public JSON that the team’s own site publishes. Nothing is sent back.
  • Vault sync ({{status_vault_reader}}). The vault reader downloads encrypted files from the vault server, dev.send.sgraph.ai. The server stores only ciphertext and opaque file names. Requests to read carry no key and no login.
  • Ask ({{status_ask}}). If you add your own OpenRouter key, your questions and the context you choose (agenda, picks, team status) are sent to openrouter.ai and nowhere else.

What stays on your device

  • Saved sessions and cached feeds. In the app’s private storage, so the reader works offline. They are not uploaded.
  • Website data. Cookies and local storage of the websites you open, such as your theme and schedule picks on PrompTugal.news. Kept apart from Safari.
  • Your website list. The Websites.json file you can see and edit in the Files app.
  • Keys ({{status_face_id}}). Vault keys and your OpenRouter key are kept in the Keychain on this device, behind Face ID, and are not synced to iCloud. They never appear in logs, the debug panels or crash reports. The debug panels show which keys exist, never their values.

Deleting the app removes all of it from the device. Data you put in your own vault stays in the vault, encrypted, until you delete it there.

Purchases

Purchases made in the app ({{status_topup}}) go through Apple. We receive no card details.

The beta

The beta is installed from Xcode or through TestFlight. If you join through TestFlight, Apple shares your email address, device model, OS version and any feedback or crash reports you send with the team. Apple’s TestFlight privacy notice has the details.

Checking this

The whole app is open source on GitHub. In debug mode, the app lists every network request it makes, with its address, size and timing.
